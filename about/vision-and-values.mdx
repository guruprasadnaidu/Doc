At Harvest International School, every decision — from curriculum design to daily routines — is guided by a deep belief that education is far more than the transfer of knowledge. We exist to grow whole persons: young people who think critically, act courageously, and engage with the world with empathy and purpose. Understanding our vision and values will help you see why Harvest is more than a school; it is a community built around the long-term flourishing of every student.

## Vision

**Consilio Et Animis — By Wisdom and Courage.**

Our motto captures the dual aspiration at the heart of everything we do. We want every student to leave Harvest not only equipped with academic knowledge, but with the wisdom to apply it well and the courage to act on it in a complex, changing world.

## Mission

We provide a holistic, inquiry-based education that develops globally conscious critical thinkers. Our mission is to create a learning environment where students are challenged to question, investigate, and construct meaning — inside the classroom and beyond it. We prepare students not merely for examinations, but for life.

We believe that genuine learning happens **both inside and outside the classroom**. Structured academic study is complemented by co-curricular experiences, community engagement, and reflective practice. Together, these shape students who are as comfortable collaborating with peers as they are presenting original ideas to the world.

## Founding Philosophy

The philosophy behind Harvest International School is rooted in the conviction that education must embrace the full spectrum of human development — **moral, emotional, physical, psychological, and spiritual** dimensions alike. Our Founder, Rev. Alan Tan, drew on the timeless insight of Aristotle, who understood that persuasion and leadership require the integration of **Ethos** (character and credibility), **Pathos** (emotional intelligence and empathy), and **Logos** (reason and rigorous thought). A student who develops all three is genuinely educated.

This vision aligns with the tradition of progressive education championed by thinkers such as **Maria Montessori** and **John Dewey**, who argued that children learn best when they are active participants in their own education — exploring, experimenting, and reflecting rather than passively receiving information. At Harvest, you will find this philosophy alive in our project-based units, student-led inquiry, and the rich co-curricular life that runs alongside formal study.

We do not treat academic performance and personal formation as separate goals. They are two sides of the same commitment: to help every student become the best version of themselves.
